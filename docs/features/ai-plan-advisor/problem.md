# Problem: choosing a plan on the pricing page

Quick mode. Source: Builder live case candidate pack (illustrative figures). Persona Nina used for traits only, not her jobs.

## User

A self-employed visitor to the pricing page who has not bought yet and must choose between Self-service (€39), Self-service + Check (€79) and Expert service (€199) for the annual return. Not a tax expert, short on time, wants to know why a plan fits. 60% of September visits (28,800 of 48,000) were on mobile.

## Job to be done

"Tell me which plan is right for me, quickly, so I can buy with confidence."

## Pain points

- **Plans are hard to tell apart.** Users say Check and Expert "sound the same" and ask who actually does the work. Self-service plus Check came with a conversion drop after launch (3 June): 13.0% in May, 10.4% in June, 11.0% in September.
- **Mobile is where it breaks.** Visit → paid is 9.2% on mobile against 13.7% on desktop. The plan comparison is hidden behind a "Compare plans" link, and users say they can't see the difference on a phone. The biggest gap is at the first step: 18.0% of mobile visitors click a plan, against 23.0% on desktop. If mobile matched desktop, that would be about 1,300 more paid purchases a month (about 3,950 vs 2,650).
- **Wrong choice leads to refunds.** 11% of Check buyers refund within 14 days, against 3–4% for the other plans. 58% of those say they "wanted an expert to do it for me".
- **Choosing takes effort.** Time on page rose from under a minute to 2:40, and "which plan" tickets rose from about 150 to about 770 a month. In the existing chat, 64% of topics are "which plan do I need".

## Success measure (proposals)

- **Visit → paid**, mobile first: move toward the 13% of April and May, and narrow the mobile/desktop gap (9.2% vs 13.7%).
- **Refund rate**, mostly on Check: down from 11% toward the 3–4% of the other plans.
- Supporting signals: fewer "which plan" tickets, shorter time to a plan decision.
- Guardrail: the advice must fit the user, not steer them to the most expensive plan.

## Out of scope

- Dropping Self-service + Check. Only a hypothesis (see open questions), not decided here.
- Checkout redesign ("hated the checkout" is noted, not addressed).
- Changing prices or plan contents.
- Any real tax advice; plans, prices and figures are illustrative.

## Open questions

- **Is Check the problem, or just badly explained?** Data can't separate them. The refund reasons suggest some buyers want Expert, but the mobile table also hides the differences.
- **Competing explanations for the drop:** the Check launch, the hidden mobile comparison, and a general drop in intent. The case data can't rank them.
- **Chat users converting at 34% vs 10.1%** are self-selected and motivated. It shows interest, not that a chat causes purchases.
- The competitor's "improved conversion" claim has no figures.
- Is a chat the right form, or would a clearer mobile comparison achieve the same? (A solution question for `/spec`.)
